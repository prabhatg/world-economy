Run a world. Help find a better one.

ManyWorlds simulates human history, from the first farmers to today, thousands of times over on volunteers' computers. Then it tests ideas that could make life better for most people, in every one of those worlds.

Why

We know a lot about what helps people. We know less about how to make it happen.

Vaccines, schooling, clean water and cash support all work. Yet billions still go without, because good ideas run into incentives, power and coordination. ManyWorlds models those forces too, so it can search for paths that actually get adopted and last.

Learn from all of history

14,000 years of farming, cities, trade, empires and industry are our training data.

Test before we try

Ideas meet thousands of simulated worlds before anyone bets real lives on them.

Include how people decide

Simulated people follow what psychology and economics know about real choices, not perfect logic.

How it works

Three steps, from the first farmers to tomorrow.

  1. STEP 1

    Learn from history

    Simulated people forage, farm, trade, migrate, fight and invent across a map of the Earth. Their history is compared with real evidence: radiocarbon dates, ancient DNA, census records.

  2. STEP 2

    Build a twin of today

    Pulled toward real data as it runs, the simulation arrives at a detailed model of the world in 2026: its people, wealth, institutions and power.

  3. STEP 3

    Test ideas in many worlds

    Each idea, like better schools or fairer taxes, runs in thousands of possible futures. We look for paths that help in nearly all of them, and for how they could be adopted.

Many worlds, one answer

An idea that works on average can still fail in many worlds.

History is chaotic, so one run proves nothing. We run every idea in many worlds and favor the ones that help nearly everywhere, especially for the poorest 40%.

Like SETI@home, for humanity

Your computer can run a world.

When your laptop, phone or browser is idle, it receives a slice of history to simulate, perhaps 500 years of one possible world, and sends back the result. A second computer repeats the work, and the results must match bit for bit.

Built on what humanity knows

Every simulated person lives inside a society and a planet.

Research from many fields enters as tested mechanisms with their sources attached. When experts disagree, we build both theories and let the evidence decide.

What “better” means

Four things we measure, for the many, not the average.

Health & longevity

Long, healthy lives and children who survive and thrive.

Material wellbeing

Enough food, a safe home, income, and less inequality.

Capabilities & freedom

Learning, a voice in decisions, and safety from violence.

Sustainability

A living planet and fair chances for people not yet born.

Honest by design

Trust has to be earned and checkable.

Open to everyone

All code, assumptions and results are public. Code is Apache-2.0; documents and results are CC-BY 4.0. A closed oracle would not deserve trust.

Uncertainty always shown

We report ranges across many worlds, never a single confident line. When a result depends on a contested theory, we say so.

Credit for everyone

Volunteers, reviewers, researchers whose papers we use and dataset creators are named in every result we publish.
